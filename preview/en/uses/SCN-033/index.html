The problem

A public institution relies on a proprietary system for records, classification, payments or decisions. Over time, staff lose knowledge of the rules and data structures. The vendor may be the only party capable of explaining anomalies or enabling migration away.

What breaks

  • Vendor Dependency
  • Opacity
  • Lock In
  • Power Asymmetry

Who holds part of the picture

  • frontline practitioners
  • domain experts
  • decision-makers
  • affected people

With Koali

Koali treats inspectability, provenance, exportability and local knowledge as governance requirements. Decisions and knowledge remain portable even when a particular software component is replaced.

Human authority

Authorized humans and institutions retain their existing legal, professional and democratic authority. Koali structures knowledge, coordination and traceability; it does not take sovereign or professional decisions.

What success looks like

  • The relevant state remains visible across handoffs and time.
  • Responsibilities, unresolved questions and escalation paths are explicit.
  • The outcome produces reusable memory for the next comparable case.

Real-world parallels

Post Office Horizon IT Inquiry

The statutory inquiry examines failures around a vendor-built accounting system whose discrepancies contributed to wrongful suspensions, terminations, prosecutions and convictions.

Relation to scenario: DIRECT

Source: https://www.postofficehorizoninquiry.org.uk/about-inquiry

Auditor General of Canada — ArriveCAN

The audit documented weak procurement records, non-competitive professional-services contracting and cases where resources were routed through intermediaries at likely additional cost.

Relation to scenario: RELATED PATTERN

Source: https://www.oag-bvg.gc.ca/internet/docs/parl_oag_202402_01_e.pdf

Boundary

The documented cases illustrate analogous failure mechanisms. They do not establish that Koali would have prevented the event or guaranteed a different outcome.
