# A Billion-Dollar Project. Nobody Can Reconstruct Why.

> Costs, requirements and risks change for years; the institution must still be able to explain each major turn.

## The problem

A major transformation produces thousands of documents and meetings. Requirements shift, costs rise, testing reveals new risks and suppliers propose changes. Years later, an auditor can find the decision but not the full chain of evidence and trade-offs that produced it.

## What breaks

- Opaque Decision
- Context Loss
- Fragmentation
- Institutional Dependency

## Who holds part of the picture

- frontline practitioners
- domain experts
- decision-makers
- affected people

## With Koali

Koali binds major decisions to the evidence, options, dissent, cost assumptions, risks, approvals and downstream work they created. The project becomes a decision graph rather than a pile of files.

## Human authority

Authorized humans and institutions retain their existing legal, professional and democratic authority. Koali structures knowledge, coordination and traceability; it does not take sovereign or professional decisions.

## What success looks like

- The relevant state remains visible across handoffs and time.
- Responsibilities, unresolved questions and escalation paths are explicit.
- The outcome produces reusable memory for the next comparable case.

## Real-world parallels

### Vérificateur général du Québec — CASA/SAAQclic

The Auditor General reported major cost growth not clearly communicated to decision-makers and weaknesses around project readiness and testing.

### Auditor General of Canada — Phoenix example

The Auditor General reiterated that Phoenix executives prioritized schedule and budget over critical elements and did not provide senior officials a complete picture of project risks.

**Relation to scenario:** `DIRECT`  
**Source:** https://www.oag-bvg.gc.ca/internet/docs/parl_oag_202310_07_e.pdf

### Auditor General of Canada — ArriveCAN

The audit documented weak procurement records, non-competitive professional-services contracting and cases where resources were routed through intermediaries at likely additional cost.

**Relation to scenario:** `STRONG ANALOGUE`  
**Source:** https://www.oag-bvg.gc.ca/internet/docs/parl_oag_202402_01_e.pdf

## Boundary

The documented cases illustrate analogous failure mechanisms. They do not establish that Koali would have prevented the event or guaranteed a different outcome.
