# The Expert Retires Friday.

> Thirty years of judgment should not leave the building in one cardboard box.

## The problem

An experienced person knows why procedures exist, which exceptions matter, which symptoms are misleading and who to call when the manual is wrong. A handover document captures tasks but often not judgment.

## What breaks

- Knowledge Attrition
- Tacit Knowledge Loss
- Single Point Of Failure
- Memory Failure

## Who holds part of the picture

- frontline practitioners
- domain experts
- decision-makers
- affected people

## With Koali

Koali captures capability through real cases: decisions, evidence, exceptions, failed approaches, conditions and outcomes. Knowledge is attached to work while the expert is still practicing, not extracted in one final interview.

## Human authority

Authorized humans and institutions retain their existing legal, professional and democratic authority. Koali structures knowledge, coordination and traceability; it does not take sovereign or professional decisions.

## What success looks like

- The relevant state remains visible across handoffs and time.
- Responsibilities, unresolved questions and escalation paths are explicit.
- The outcome produces reusable memory for the next comparable case.

## Real-world parallels

### IAEA — Nuclear Knowledge Management

The IAEA treats retirement and loss of critical knowledge as explicit organizational risks requiring succession planning and knowledge-loss assessment.

**Relation to scenario:** `DIRECT`  
**Source:** https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub1724_web.pdf

### U.S. GAO — Critical Legacy Systems

GAO identified critical federal legacy systems 8 to 51 years old, including outdated languages, unsupported technology and known vulnerabilities.

**Relation to scenario:** `RELATED PATTERN`  
**Source:** https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-19-471

## Boundary

The documented cases illustrate analogous failure mechanisms. They do not establish that Koali would have prevented the event or guaranteed a different outcome.

`signals → shared meaning → evidence → choice → responsibility → action → verification → memory → better knowledge`
