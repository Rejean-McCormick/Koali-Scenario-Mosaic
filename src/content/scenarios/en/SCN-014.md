# The Expertise Exists. The Right People Never Meet.

> Complex problems are often organized by department even when the solution requires a temporary community of expertise.

## The problem

A problem spans engineering, law, operations, economics and lived experience. Each institution knows people in its own network, but no one has a reliable way to discover the full set of relevant expertise or to distinguish authority by domain.

## What breaks

- Expertise Isolation
- Organizational Silo
- Routing Failure
- Fragmentation

## Who holds part of the picture

- researchers
- funders
- reviewers
- future teams

## With Koali

Koali routes problems to domain-bounded expertise, makes evidence of expertise inspectable, assembles temporary cross-domain teams and keeps each contribution connected to its field, assumptions and conflicts of interest.

## Human authority

Authorized humans and institutions retain their existing legal, professional and democratic authority. Koali structures knowledge, coordination and traceability; it does not take sovereign or professional decisions.

## What success looks like

- The relevant state remains visible across handoffs and time.
- Responsibilities, unresolved questions and escalation paths are explicit.
- The outcome produces reusable memory for the next comparable case.

## Real-world parallels

### FDA — History of the agency’s role in HIV/AIDS

The FDA documents how AIDS activists pushed regulators toward faster access and more direct patient participation in policy and advisory processes.

**Relation to scenario:** `STRONG ANALOGUE`

### Auditor General of Canada — ArriveCAN

The audit documented weak procurement records, non-competitive professional-services contracting and cases where resources were routed through intermediaries at likely additional cost.

## Boundary

The documented cases illustrate analogous failure mechanisms. They do not establish that Koali would have prevented the event or guaranteed a different outcome.
