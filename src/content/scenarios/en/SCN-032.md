# The Government Keeps Buying Back Its Own Knowledge.

> Outside expertise should leave the public institution more capable, not more dependent.

## The problem

A government launches a large programme after years of turnover and fragmented internal knowledge. External specialists are hired to reconstruct architecture, requirements or strategy. Their expertise may be excellent; the structural problem appears when the contract ends and the understanding leaves too.

## What breaks

- Institutional Dependency
- Knowledge Attrition
- Vendor Dependency
- Provenance Loss

## Who holds part of the picture

- public servants
- leaders
- auditors
- vendors

## With Koali

Koali makes external work contribute to a governed institutional knowledge base: assumptions, architecture, decisions, evidence, lessons, reusable artifacts and named internal stewards. Expertise can come from outside without institutional memory being outsourced with it.

## Human authority

Authorized humans and institutions retain their existing legal, professional and democratic authority. Koali structures knowledge, coordination and traceability; it does not take sovereign or professional decisions.

## What success looks like

- The relevant state remains visible across handoffs and time.
- Responsibilities, unresolved questions and escalation paths are explicit.
- The outcome produces reusable memory for the next comparable case.
