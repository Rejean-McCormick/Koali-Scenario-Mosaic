# A Charismatic Claim Travels Faster Than the Evidence.

> One confident voice can look like one side of a debate even when the underlying evidence is profoundly asymmetric.

## The problem

A persuasive speaker presents a simple claim. The public sees a person versus another person, not the structure of evidence behind them. A minority position can gain apparent parity because attention is easier to observe than evidentiary weight.

## What breaks

- Noise Amplification
- False Balance
- Provenance Loss
- Expertise Mismatch

## Who holds part of the picture

- public health
- clinicians
- community organizations
- affected people

Koali publishes a consensus map at the level of claims: what is strongly supported, what is disputed, what is unknown, what risks are recognized, which evidence supports each statement, and where credible minority views remain.

## Real-world parallels

### National Academies — Vaccines and Autism

The 2004 consensus review found that epidemiological evidence favored rejection of a causal relationship between MMR vaccination and autism.

**Relation to scenario:** `DIRECT`  
**Source:** https://www.nationalacademies.org/publications/10997

### CDC — Surgeon General’s Reports on Smoking and Health

The 1964 Surgeon General’s report synthesized more than 7,000 publications and concluded that smoking causes serious disease.

**Relation to scenario:** `STRONG ANALOGUE`  
**Source:** https://cdc.gov/tobacco-surgeon-general-reports/about/history.html

### IPCC AR6 Working Group I — Summary for Policymakers

The IPCC synthesizes multiple lines of evidence with explicit confidence language and states that human influence has unequivocally warmed the atmosphere, ocean and land.

**Relation to scenario:** `STRONG ANALOGUE`  
**Source:** https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/summary-for-policymakers/
